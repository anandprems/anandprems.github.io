Nominal Data — Definition, Examples, and When to Use

In the previous blog, we looked at Qualitative vs Quantitative Data.

Now let's go one step deeper.

Imagine you have a list of students:

  • CSE
  • ECE
  • EEE
  • Mechanical

You can group the students based on their department.

But can you say that CSE is greater than ECE?

Of course not.

These are simply categories.

And this is where Nominal Data comes in.

What Is Nominal Data?

Nominal data is data used to name or label categories.

The word nominal comes from the idea of names or labels.

The important point is:

Nominal data has categories, but those categories do not have a meaningful order or ranking.

For example:

Blood Group

A+, A−, B+, B−, AB+, AB−, O+, O−

O+ is not "greater" than A+. They are simply different categories.

Some Everyday Examples

Think about the information we encounter every day:

Example Nominal Categories
Department CSE, ECE, EEE, ME
Blood Group A+, B+, AB+, O+
Eye Colour Brown, Blue, Green
Country India, Japan, USA
Type of Device Laptop, Tablet, Mobile
Favourite Programming Language C, Python, Java
Payment Method Cash, Card, UPI

In each case, the values are labels.

There is no natural "higher" or "lower" category.

What About Numbers?

Here is something interesting.

Nominal data can sometimes look like numbers.

Suppose a college gives students these department codes:

1 → CSE
2 → ECE
3 → EEE

Are the students with department code 3 somehow "greater" than those with department code 1?

No.

The numbers are simply labels.

So don't look at the format alone.

Does the number represent a quantity, or is it just a label?

When Do We Use Nominal Data?

We use nominal data when we want to:

Identify → Categorize → Group → Count

For example, a college might want to know:

How many students are from each department?

A hospital might want to know:

How many patients belong to each blood group?

An online store might ask:

Which payment method do customers use most often?

In each case, we are working with categories.

One Simple Rule

If you can say:

"These are different types, but one is not greater than another."

You are probably looking at Nominal Data.

Think: Nominal = Name

It tells us what category something belongs to.

It doesn't tell us how much.

It doesn't tell us which is higher or lower.

It simply gives us a label.

Why Does This Matter in Data Science?

Because before analysing a dataset, we need to understand what each column actually represents.

Consider:

Age → 21, 22, 23

That's quantitative data.

But:

Department → CSE, ECE, EEE

That's categorical data.

Treating categories as if they were numerical measurements can lead to incorrect analysis.

So before asking:

"What can I calculate from this data?"

ask:

"What does this data actually represent?"

That small question can prevent a big mistake.

Remember

Nominal Data = Names / Labels

No meaningful order.
No ranking.
Just categories.

CSE ≠ ECE

Not because one is greater or smaller, but simply because they are different categories.

And that's the idea behind nominal data.
