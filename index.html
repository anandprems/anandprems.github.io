Publications

My research really began once I enrolled for my PhD — before that, it was scattered projects with UG students, fun but unfocused. Once the domain settled around deep learning, transformers, and their applications to ECG signals and medical images, it turned into a genuinely beautiful journey to be on. None of it would have started without Dr. S. Sathiya Narayanan, Associate Professor at VIT Chennai, who first opened up the world of coding to me — and through that, AI. I wouldn't call myself an expert, just someone who loves exploring — these papers are where that exploration has landed so far. Have a look, and if something here resonates, I'd genuinely love to collaborate.

Research

A PhD isn't just another degree stacked after UG and PG — it's meant to make you capable of thinking about something genuinely novel: finding real research gaps and exploring them with independence, rather than being told what to work on. That's really where my individual research life began. My move from Electronics into Computational Intelligence, and later into teaching, kept opening up new things to explore, one at a time. Here's where that exploration stands right now.
Ongoing · started Sep 2026

Tamil-language interface for drone control

Translating "Drone Technology for Beginners" into Tamil — a project acquired by the Tamil Computing research domain, Department of Computational Intelligence, SRM Institute of Science and Technology, for the Government of Tamil Nadu, so the material isn't gated behind English fluency. Early days, but the direction is promising.

Submitted Sep 2026

A survey on deep learning in oncology

Started Jul 2026. A survey chapter mapping how deep learning techniques are being applied across cancer diagnostics — submitted as a book chapter for a Scopus-indexed Springer publication. More details once it clears review.

Ongoing · started Sep 2026

12-lead ECG vs. single-lead wearable ECG

Can a single-lead signal from something as small as an Apple Watch hold its own against the clinical 12-lead gold standard? Early results are... interesting. Watch this space.

Guest Lectures

People tend to assume a guest lecture means showing up as an "expert" — that's not really how it happens for me. I'm constantly exploring, both academically and through research, and that process never really stops — every new project brings new technologies I have to catch up on myself. Naturally, I end up sharing what I pick up, either as a blog post or a short video. Somewhere along the way, sharing it with colleagues sparked enough curiosity that a few of them started inviting me over for guest lectures. What keeps me going back, though, isn't the "talk" itself — it's getting to meet different minds, faculty and students alike, and fielding questions from angles I hadn't considered. I usually end up understanding the concept better myself after those conversations. I'm just genuinely happy to be part of that back-and-forth of sharing knowledge.

About

I am an educator and AI researcher with a passion for teaching, learning, and exploring how technology can solve meaningful problems. Over the years, my journey has moved across teaching, biomedical processing, artificial intelligence, and deep learning, while always keeping learning at the centre.

I enjoy simplifying complex concepts, experimenting with new ideas, guiding students through their learning journey, and sharing what I learn through code, articles, and videos.

For me, education is not just about completing a syllabus — it is about understanding why something matters, applying what we learn, exploring beyond the classroom, and developing the confidence to create something of our own.

Academic Journey

I wasn't a topper — just an average student who loved biology but never had any interest in medicine, even after I got the marks for it. Microbiology was where I actually wanted to go, but my parents insisted on engineering, and I went along with it. UG turned out fine either way. A close friend, Surya Prakash, once wrote in my slam book on our last day: "A person who goes happily without thinking about his future" — and looking back, that was true.

The spark for who I wanted to become actually came during my PG, through my guide and mentor — my Gurunathar, Dr. A. Selvaraj, Professor at Rajalakshmi Engineering College. We all imagine different professions for ourselves growing up, but teaching was honestly never one of mine — until I saw him. He showed me that a professor isn't some distant, aliened figure; they're human too, with a soft side once they step out of the classroom. All credit for pulling me toward teaching goes to him. The second person who pushed and encouraged me toward this field is my wife, Dr. S. Santhalakshmi. It all began in 2011, and there were many good-hearted well-wishers along the way — my whole-hearted thanks to all of them.

And above everyone, Dr. APJ Abdul Kalam — the main reason above all. A man who achieved so much and still stayed so humble, who genuinely loved teaching, who answered every question in the simplest, most childlike, innocent way. That, more than anything, is what motivates me to be in this profession.
